How to organise a community cleanup (step by step)

A cleanup is one of the most visible, rewarding ways to improve a place and bring neighbours together. You do not need an organisation behind you - just a plan.

Step 1: Choose the place and time

Look for a spot that has visible litter and is safe to reach, such as a beach, riverbank, park or neighbourhood street. Pick a daylight time with good weather - for beaches, check the tide chart and aim for low tide.

Step 2: Get permission and support

Contact the local authority, park manager or landowner. Ask whether they provide bags, gloves or pickers and whether they will collect the full bags. Many councils support cleanups and can advise on hazards.

Step 3: Gather volunteers

Share the date on community groups, schools, sports clubs and social media. Ask people to sign up so you know how many to expect. Ask about accessibility needs so everyone can take part.

Step 4: Prepare supplies and safety

Use gloves, bags, pickers, a first-aid kit, water and sun protection. Brief everyone on safety: never touch needles, chemicals or large broken glass; stay away from water edges and traffic; and supervise children. Our cleanup planner has a full checklist you can print.

Step 5: Run the event

Welcome people, explain the safety rules, split into zones with team leaders, and sort recyclables if possible. Take photos of before and after. Count or weigh what you collect - noting the top items helps campaign for change.

Step 6: Dispose and follow up

Dispose of waste as agreed. Thank volunteers, share results (without personal details) and report data to citizen-science projects. Schedule the next date while enthusiasm is high.

Common mistakes

  • Overfilling bags - they become too heavy to lift safely.
  • Skipping the safety briefing.
  • Forgetting to arrange waste collection.
  • Cleaning up sensitive habitats such as dunes or nesting areas without guidance.
