A calm 5-minute daily news routine

Constant news alerts can leave us anxious yet no better informed. A short, deliberate routine gives you the essentials and your attention back.

The routine

  1. Minute 1 - Weather and day ahead. Check the forecast and anything that affects your commute or plans.
  2. Minutes 2-3 - Top headlines. Scan the main stories from a few different reputable sources. Read only the headlines and, for one or two stories that matter to you, the first paragraphs.
  3. Minute 4 - One topic you care about. Technology, science, business, health or your local area.
  4. Minute 5 - Something to learn or enjoy. This day in history, a featured article, or a long read saved for later.

Make it stick

  • Use a fixed time (with the first coffee) and a fixed place so it becomes a habit.
  • Turn off most news notifications; keep only genuine safety alerts.
  • Pick two or three sources with different viewpoints and a reputation for corrections.
  • Decide a stop point. News has no end; your attention does.

Why it helps

Research on media habits has linked heavy, repeated exposure to negative news with higher stress. Most breaking news is also revised within hours, so waiting for a daily summary often gives you a more accurate picture.

Do something with what you read

If a story bothers you, choose one small action: donate, volunteer, contact a representative, or help a neighbour. Agency is the best antidote to news anxiety.
