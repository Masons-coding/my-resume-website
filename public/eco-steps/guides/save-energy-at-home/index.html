Save energy at home: the biggest wins first

Heating and cooling usually account for around half of a household's energy use, followed by water heating, appliances and lighting. Focusing on the big items saves more than hunting for tiny savings.

1. Heating and cooling

  • Lower the thermostat by 1-2 degrees in winter (and raise it in summer). Each degree can save a few percent on heating costs.
  • Use a programmable or smart thermostat to reduce heating when you are asleep or away.
  • Seal drafts around doors and windows; add insulation in the attic, which is often the cheapest big upgrade.
  • Service your furnace or heat pump and replace filters regularly. Heat pumps are far more efficient than electric resistance heating.

2. Hot water

  • Take shorter showers and fit a low-flow showerhead.
  • Turn your water heater down to about 50-55°C (120-130°F); do not go below 50°C (risk of bacteria) and do not set it so high that it scalds.
  • Wash clothes in cold water - detergent works well and most of a washing machine's energy goes to heating the water.

3. Lighting and appliances

  • Switch to LED bulbs: they use about 75-80% less energy than old incandescent bulbs and last much longer.
  • Unplug or use power strips for devices with standby drain: games consoles, TVs, chargers, old computers.
  • When replacing appliances, check the energy rating (such as ENERGY STAR).
  • Air-dry clothes when you can and run full loads in the dishwasher.

4. The fridge

Keep the fridge at about 3-4°C and the freezer at -18°C, vacuum the coils, and check the door seal. An old second fridge in the garage can be a surprisingly large energy drain.

5. Think big: electricity source and transport

If your area allows, choose renewable electricity or install solar. For transport, walking, cycling, public transit, car-sharing and electric vehicles reduce emissions significantly.
